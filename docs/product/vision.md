# Vision

## The problem

Before building a part of a structure, a contractor must have its plans, calculation notes and
method statements approved by several parties: an architect, one or more engineering offices, a
control office. Today the approvals are tracked in a spreadsheet that circulates.

What goes wrong, observed on a real site's spreadsheet:

- there is no date of reply, so a delay cannot be proven;
- a status is a cell that gets overwritten, so there is no history;
- versions of the same plan are separate lines linked only by a file name;
- "no answer" is typed by hand, and nobody sees who is late, or who was never concerned;
- comments are lost in cells or in attached files.

## What Paraphe is

A place where the contractor sends a revision of a document to the parties who must answer, each
answers through a personal link without an account, and everything is recorded in a journal that
cannot be rewritten. The state of every document, and who is holding it up, is visible at a glance.

## Who it is for

- **The contractor** (first user: a contractor on a heritage restoration site in Morocco). Wants to
  know where every document stands and to prove who answered what, and when.
- **Reviewers** (architect, engineering offices, control office). Want to open a plan and answer in
  a minute, from a phone, without creating an account.

## What makes it different from a shared drive

1. **The status is derived**, not typed, from who answered what on which revision.
2. **Only the people asked can be late.** An office that is not concerned by a document is never
   shown as overdue.
3. **The proof is built in.** Opened, viewed, answered, revoked: all journaled, immutably.
4. **No friction for reviewers.** A link is enough.

## What we will not do

- Replace the reviewers' own tools or their drawing software.
- Review the content of a plan: Paraphe tracks approvals, not engineering.
- Give reviewers accounts (not in the first versions).
- Become a general document management system.

## How we will know it works

The contractor runs a real project for two weeks without reopening the spreadsheet, and can prove,
with dates, who answered what on which revision.
